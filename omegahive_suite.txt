Status. This document is a detailed specified design and experimental programme. It consolidates
the evaluation architecture developed for comparing OmegaClaw hives whose agents contain different
combinations of PRIMUS, predictive-coding, memory, reasoning, attention, motivational, transfer,
self-modeling, and collective-control processes. Numerical thresholds and implementation schedules
are illustrative unless backed by a versioned experiment package. The suite is intended to make
architectural progress cumulative and falsifiable; it is not a claim that any one score, environment, or
present system establishes general intelligence.

The OmegaHive ProtoAGI Test Suite

August 2026

Abstract
The OmegaHive ProtoAGI Test Suite is designed to answer a practical research question: when
a coding agent adds cognitive process X, Y , or Z to an OmegaClaw agent or hive, did the addition
produce a real, generalizable improvement, did it interact constructively with the rest of the architecture,
and was the gain worth its computational and organizational cost? The suite is therefore structured as
a validation ecology rather than a leaderboard benchmark. It combines inexpensive diagnostic microworlds, persistent developmental curricula, procedurally generated promotion tests, sentinel holdouts,
formal and software environments with exact oracles, simulated social and scientific worlds, multi-agent
project factories, an OmegaSelf governance twin, an Isaac-style embodied robotics laboratory, and a
cross-domain transfer ring. Its common evaluator records predictions before actions, authenticated
outcomes, learning-state deltas, provenance, compute, and sampled causal ablations. Architectural
variants are compared through matched-seed factorial experiments, developmental learning curves, order
tests, protected-anchor regressions, and staged shadow promotion. Evaluation episodes become learning
material after their scores are sealed, so the system is trained by much of the same experience that
validates it. The intended result is not a single “AGI score,” but a continuously updated generality
frontier covering breadth, novelty tolerance, learning efficiency, retention, transfer, metacognition,
collective intelligence, governance, and resource efficiency.

1 Executive Summary
1.1 The object being evaluated
The central experimental object is not a bare language model and not a fixed monolithic agent. It is a
versioned system of the form
“An OmegaClaw agent or OmegaHive in which some specified set of PRIMUS and predictivecoding processes has been integrated, at a specified locus, with a specified authority and
resource envelope.”
Examples include an OmegaClaw agent with predictive coding, HMH episodic memory, and PLN; a hive
whose chief-of-staff uses TECAN and SubRep while individual agents use WILLIAM-guided retrieval; or a
robotics hive in which a shared causal world-model service is advisory while local agents retain action
authority. The evaluation system must therefore distinguish at least four questions:
1. Did the modified system accomplish more, more reliably, more quickly, or more economically?
2. Which added process, interaction, or placement caused the improvement?
3. Did the experience produce reusable knowledge, skills, calibration, or coordination patterns?
4. What regressions, interference, governance failures, coordination burdens, or tail risks accompanied
the gain?
This framing follows Hyperon’s cognitive-synergy thesis: different processes should share structured
intermediate states and cooperate when useful, yet remain modular enough to benchmark, shadow,
promote, retain as fallbacks, and roll back [1]. It also follows the action-first world-model criterion: an
internal representation is useful insofar as it supports calibrated prediction of how the world, the agent, or
the representation will change under external or cognitive actions.

Use AGI Maze as the kernel, not the ceiling; use the whole ecology to test

2 Purpose, Scope, and Design Principles
2.1 Why a validation ecology rather than a conventional benchmark
The goal is not primarily to obtain an externally impressive score. It is to help the architecture improve
without deceiving its developers. A fixed benchmark with one aggregate score creates several pathologies:
• coding agents learn to optimize the visible cases rather than the intended capability;
• architectural processes are credited for gains caused by an unrelated model route or tool;
• a narrow gain can conceal catastrophic forgetting or governance regressions;
• evaluation burns compute without creating durable knowledge;
• the system can become better at describing its own success without becoming better at acting;
• average performance can conceal rare but severe loops, permission failures, or runaway cost.

2

The OmegaHive ProtoAGI Test Suite

August 2026

A validation ecology instead contains related but non-identical environments, developmental seasons, hidden
generators, real work, causal ablations, and explicit promotion rules. It measures both final outcomes and
the trajectories that produced them.

2.2 Primary design requirements
The suite is governed by the following requirements.
2.2.1 Cognitive breadth
The environments must jointly exercise perception, state estimation, memory, reasoning, causal induction,
attention, planning, motivation, option learning, program discovery, transfer, social cognition, self-modeling,
governance, and multi-agent coordination. No single environment is expected to cover all of these cleanly.
2.2.2 Progress toward generality rather than benchmark specialization
The core evidence for generality is performance under structural novelty, rapid adaptation, retention,
selective transfer, and cross-domain reuse. Merely increasing the score on familiar instances is classified as
task-local improvement until it survives stronger tests.
2.2.3 Low routine computational cost
Most per-commit tests should use symbolic, graph, textual, trace-replay, or simplified-physics environments.
Frontier models, sensor-rich simulation, long multi-agent projects, and physical-robot trials belong at later
gates.
2.2.4 Learning value
Where possible, evaluation should produce reusable episodes, rules, skills, options, calibration records,
failure precedents, proof motifs, causal models, and coordination templates. Hidden promotion experiences
are released into the curriculum only after scoring is sealed.
2.2.5 Automated or externally grounded scoring
Deterministic scripts, environment ground truth, formal proof checkers, tests, simulators, provenance closure,
and replay should provide most scores. Human judgment remains important for scientific significance,
conceptual novelty, social appropriateness, and artifact quality, but should be a sampled audit rather than
the routine acceptance mechanism. LLM judges may triage; they should not be the sole authority for
architectural promotion.
2.2.6 Causal attribution
Every proposed process must have a predeclared causal signature and required ablations. High activity,
low internal energy, many discovered patterns, or persuasive explanations are not enough. The process
must measurably alter predictions, actions, artifacts, learning curves, or resource allocation in the expected
direction.

2.2.7 Governed cumulative change
Evaluation is connected to OmegaClaw’s module lifecycle: benchmark, shadow, promote, monitor, retain a
fallback, and roll back. The suite is part of the self-modification pipeline rather than an after-the-fact

3 Architectural Context
3.1 Hyperon, PRIMUS, OmegaClaw, OmegaSelf, and OmegaHive
Hyperon provides a shared metagraph substrate and a family of heterogeneous cognitive algorithms.
PRIMUS organizes those processes into goal-directed and ambient cognition. OmegaClaw provides the
deployable control fabric: Module Spaces, Context Frames, planning, attention, routing, monitoring, and
rollback. OmegaSelf adds evidence-grounded capability beliefs, pre-action prediction, typed proposals, a
separately authorized policy gate, and directed continuity. OmegaHive extends the system across multiple
agents with explicit task ownership, shared and individual memory, provenance, communication tiers,
permissions, and recovery [1].
The test suite should preserve these distinctions:
• PRIMUS processes are hypotheses about cognition and cognitive synergy.
• OmegaClaw determines how those processes are made operational and replaceable.
• OmegaSelf determines how the running system makes and governs claims about itself.
• OmegaHive determines how cognition is distributed across socially and operationally distinct agents.
• OmegaEval determines what evidence is sufficient to change trust, authority, or deployment status.

3.2 The migration unit is a capability behind a Module Space
A process should not be introduced as an uninspectable edit to the whole hive. It should normally appear
as a versioned capability behind a Module Space or a clearly delimited internal process. The evaluator
records its type, implementation, inputs and outputs, location, cost, latency, authority, health, and fallback.
This allows an incumbent stand-in and a new PRIMUS component to run side by side.

3.3 Context Frames as evaluation state
A Context Frame is the durable, typed state of an active cognitive episode, not merely a prompt. For
evaluation purposes it should contain:
• goals, constraints, motives, permissions, and completion criteria;
• active map, entity, causal, social, and self-capability hypotheses;
• evidence, provenance, staleness, and dependence groups;
• candidate external and cognitive actions;
• predicted outcomes, costs, risks, reversibility, and information gain;
• plans, branches, module assignments, and resource budgets;
• action receipts, discrepancies, learning updates, and artifact references;
• the evaluation record explaining why the episode is complete or incomplete.
A Frame is good when it improves calibrated, low-cost action-conditioned prediction and decision quality.
A large Frame containing true but behaviorally irrelevant facts is cognitive clutter.

6.2 A hierarchy of novelty
A practical novelty ladder is:
1. fresh instances under familiar rules;
2. unfamiliar combinations of known skills or mechanics;
3. changed causal rules under the same representation;
4. familiar structure expressed through a new vocabulary, ontology, API, sensor suite, or modality;
5. changed goals, risks, permissions, or motive tradeoffs;
6. new partners, authority relations, hive topology, or body configuration;
7. cross-domain renderings whose latent structure is preserved but whose surface ontology and action

Results should be automatically classified into evidence levels.

7.2 Larger architectural spaces
For more than three processes, exhaustive factorial evaluation quickly becomes expensive. The suite should
use fractional-factorial designs, covering arrays, or Bayesian experimental design to screen main effects and
low-order interactions. Full ablations are then concentrated around promising or dangerous regions.

7.3 Frozen-state and developmental modes
Every important comparison should be run in two modes.
Frozen-state mode disables or quarantines learning. It measures immediate inference, routing, retrieval,
planning, and control effects.
Developmental mode starts each variant from the same snapshot and exposes it to the same sequence
of tasks while learning. The score is the full learning trajectory, final competence, retention, transfer, and
knowledge dividend.
A component that raises immediate performance but destroys cumulative learning is not an architectural
win.

Activation is not explanation. The evaluator should periodically mask a memory, rule, template, column,
PC connection, scheduler, option, message, or agent role and rerun the same seed. Exact ablation is
expensive, so learned or information-geometric impact surrogates may operate at scale, but they must be
recalibrated against exact interventions.

• candidate world model plus an oracle planner;
• oracle world model plus the candidate planner;
• candidate predictor under a fixed exploration policy;
• fixed state estimator plus the candidate attention or metareasoning controller;
• fixed low-level robot controller plus candidate high-level cognition;
• candidate decomposition plus fixed specialist workers, and vice versa.
These tests clarify whether a gain comes from representation, planning, control, communication, perception,

8 Evaluation as a Learning Process
8.1 Three lanes
8.1.1 Curriculum lane
Tasks, environment mechanics, solutions, and failure traces are visible. Agents are encouraged to learn,
consolidate, form skills, improve routing, and mine patterns. These are persistent schools rather than
disposable test questions.
8.1.2 Promotion lane
The environment family is known, but concrete worlds, structural transformations, seeds, and some
mechanics are private. The system may learn within an episode because adaptation is part of intelligence.
Ground truth and score details remain sealed until the promotion decision is complete.
8.1.3 Sentinel lane
Generator implementations or transformation families are not exposed to development agents. Some cases
are instantiated only after candidate code and configuration are frozen. Sentinel tests are used sparingly
for releases, major self-modifications, and evaluator audits.

8.2 Release after sealing
After a promotion episode is scored and the decision is sealed, most of its traces can enter the curriculum
lane. Failed plans, successful experiments, calibration records, discovered affordances, proof obstructions,
repair procedures, and coordination patterns become future training material. Freshness is maintained by
generating new structures rather than permanently withholding all experience.

8.3 Hold out structures, not item identifiers
Renaming objects in a memorized task is not a meaningful holdout. Promotion tests should hold out
causal topology, mechanic combinations, observation models, goal structures, body configurations, social
institutions, software dependency patterns, or proof transformations.

8.5 Dense developmental seasons

10 AGI Maze: The Abstract World-Modeling Kernel
10.1 Why AGI Maze should be central
AGI Maze is a lightweight, low-bandwidth, interactive framework designed to test whether an agent
constructs and uses a persistent representation of a hidden, evolving world under partial observability [2].
It intentionally removes pixel-level perception and continuous control so that state representation, memory,
uncertainty, mapping, localization, and planning become the primary bottlenecks.
This makes AGI Maze particularly well matched to architectural CI. It is inexpensive enough to run
thousands of paired candidate-versus-baseline episodes. Its latent state is known exactly to the evaluator.
Its mechanics can be procedurally composed. It can support belief probes and oracle substitutions. Most
importantly, it is difficult enough to expose the brittleness of raw prompt history while remaining simple
enough for strong algorithmic baselines.
The appropriate interpretation is:
15

The OmegaHive ProtoAGI Test Suite

August 2026

AGI Maze is a world-modeling microscope; Neoterics is a cognitive ecology.
The microscope should be used constantly. The ecology should test whether what it reveals matters in
richer settings.

10.2 Base environment
The base framework uses a hidden grid with internal walls, an unbreakable border or monolith with an
exit, a key, and a treasure chest. The agent receives the grid size, start state, available actions, inventory,
and textual or structured observations, but not the map. It must track location, walls, passages, visited
and unvisited cells, object state, and goal preconditions.
Dynamic mechanics increase partial observability:
• Rivers cause forced downstream movement with initially unknown flow direction.
• Pit cycles transport the agent among hidden pit locations.
• Limited step budgets make systematic but wasteful exploration insufficient.
• Goal dependencies require keys, treasure, exit credentials, or strict action sequences.
These mechanics create observation histories compatible with several current locations. A robust agent
therefore needs a distribution, set, or structured family of hypotheses rather than one confidently asserted
coordinate.

10.3 Existing task groups
The framework’s task groups provide a useful starting structure [2]:
• TUTORIAL: open maps and direct teaching; not scored.
• TRAINING: small mazes with generous budgets for iteration and learning.
• CLASSIC: larger core-rule mazes calibrated against competent human play.
• EXTENDED: new items, cell types, and actions requiring new strategies.
• HIDDEN: private holdouts containing unfamiliar mechanics that may need to be inferred from
interaction.
OmegaEval should preserve these groups but add continual-learning seasons, multi-objective tasks, multiagent variants, structural generator holdouts, and world-model probes.

10.4 What the initial paper indicates
The reported baselines show that vanilla LLM agents often fail to solve even small training mazes reliably.
Stronger models improve when allowed to write working-memory notes, but the resulting text-based state
remains brittle and generally does not become a complete explicit map [2]. The important conclusion
for this suite is not that LLMs cannot solve mazes, but that persistent state and representation are not
guaranteed by next-token inference over raw interaction history.
These results establish a useful difficulty regime, but they do not establish a PRIMUS advantage. Strong
conventional state-estimation and planning baselines are essential.

11 Neoterics: The Broader Abstract Cognitive Ecology
11.1 Role in the suite
Neoterics should not be discarded when AGI Maze becomes the low-cost kernel. It serves a different
purpose. AGI Maze isolates representation and planning. Neoterics tests whether many kinds of cognition
coexist in a persistent, changing ecology.
A first implementation may be graph based, tile based, or lightweight 2D rather than photorealistic. The
world contains:
• objects with initially unknown affordances;
• recipes and multi-step transformations;
• limited resources, energy, and deadlines;
• hazards and irreversible actions;
• tools with hidden preconditions and failure modes;
• spatial, temporal, institutional, and social constraints;
• other agents with whom one can trade, cooperate, teach, negotiate, or compete;
• periodic changes to rules, ontology, observation format, or market conditions.
The evaluator knows the true state and causal rules while the candidate receives text, symbols, simplified
pixels, or mixed observations.

11.2 Core task generators
1. Unknown affordance discovery: infer what a new object, material, or device does and under
which context.
2. Build-and-barter: acquire resources, transform them through a recipe, negotiate exchange, and
satisfy cost or norm constraints.
3. Repair and adaptation: diagnose why a known procedure failed after a world update and construct
a corrected option.

20

The OmegaHive ProtoAGI Test Suite

August 2026

4. Ecological planning: achieve a long-horizon goal while conserving scarce resources and avoiding
delayed hazards.
5. Active observation: choose sensor, query, experiment, or viewpoint actions for their expected
decision value.
6. Teaching and cultural transfer: communicate an acquired skill or norm to another agent and
assess whether it learned the intended structure.
7. Institutional action: operate under permissions, contracts, ownership, commitments, and changing
social roles.

11.3 Metrics
Primary metrics include task success, action-prediction calibration, interventions required to discover an
affordance, information gained per experiment, planning regret, staleness recovery, option composition,
resource use, delayed consequences, retention, selective transfer, social commitment compliance, and
whether the system can identify which evidence revised its model.

11.4 Why Neoterics remains important
A purpose-built map and POMDP solver can cover much of AGI Maze. Neoterics asks whether the hive
can decide which representation, module, skill, motive, and social strategy to use when the environment is
not organized around one navigation problem. It is therefore a stronger test of cognitive ecology, but a
weaker microscope for attributing one component’s effect.

14 LeanGarden: Formal Mathematics and Automated Conjecturing
14.1 Purpose and authority
LeanGarden evaluates formal reasoning, conjecture generation, lemma invention, representation change,
counterexample search, and long-horizon proof strategy. Exact proof status is determined by independent
replay in a pinned Lean environment. Learned scores, LLM criticism, PLN belief, or an attractive informal
argument may guide search but cannot close a formal claim.
This clean separation between heuristic guidance and exact authority is particularly valuable for architectural
evaluation. It prevents verbal plausibility from masquerading as correctness.

14.2 Problem sources
The environment should mix:
• generated theorem families with known structure and difficulty controls;
• small isolated Lean exercises;
• evolving formal libraries containing reusable lemmas;
• selected real projects with pinned dependencies and exact statements;
• paired informal and formal statements requiring alignment review;
• false conjectures and near-miss statements for counterexample detection.

14.3 Episode types
1. Interpret a literal statement and construct a formalization plan.
2. Prove a theorem under a fixed premise set and resource budget.
3. Invent a bridge lemma from a persistent residual proof state.

15 Virtual Scientist: Causal Discovery and Experiment Design
15.1 Purpose
Virtual Scientist evaluates whether the hive can form mechanistic hypotheses, distinguish causal from
correlational structure, choose discriminating interventions, update under noisy evidence, and generate
reusable scientific models. Routine CI should use synthetic but scientifically structured worlds so the
evaluator knows the causal truth. Real datasets provide external-validity and practical shadow streams.

15.2 World families
Candidate generators include:
• causal graphs with interventions, latent confounders, selection bias, and regime changes;
• biochemical-style reaction and signaling networks;
• ecological population and resource systems;
• materials, manufacturing, and process-control systems;
• small dynamical systems with hidden variables and noisy measurements;
• scientific literature graphs containing correlated, duplicated, stale, and contradictory evidence.

15.3 Scientific episode protocol
An episode may provide observational data, a budget of experiments, a set of available interventions, and
a target such as mechanism identification, prediction under intervention, or selection of a robust treatment
or process setting. The hive produces:
• a ranked hypothesis set with calibrated uncertainty;
• an explicit evidence and dependence account;
• predicted outcomes of candidate experiments;
• a selected experiment and rationale;
• an updated model after observation;
• counterevidence and failure conditions;
• reusable causal motifs or programs if discovered.

15.4 Metrics
• held-out observational and interventional predictive likelihood;
• causal-edge, mechanism, or latent-state recovery where identifiable;
• intervention regret and information gained per experimental cost;

The OmegaHive ProtoAGI Test Suite

August 2026

• calibration and diversity of hypotheses;
• replication on fresh generated worlds;
• speed and correctness of recovery after a mechanism changes;
• duplicate-evidence and correlated-source accounting;
• value of discovered rules or mechanisms on later worlds;

15.5 Synthetic and real science
Biology is a strategically useful surface because genes, proteins, pathways, phenotypes, interventions, and
literature form rich graphs. However, prospective scientific correctness is often not immediately available.
Promotion gates should therefore rely primarily on hidden synthetic mechanisms, retrospective prediction,
held-out interventions, and reproducible analyses. Real bioinformatics, materials, or other domain work
belongs in the shadow stream and can produce hypotheses for later empirical testing.

15.6 Testing semantic chemistry and discovery loops
A discovery loop is not rewarded merely because it generates many mechanisms. Paired replay, rule
masking, chamber intervention, and active-set ablation should estimate whether a proposed pattern or
reaction network changes predictive likelihood, intervention choice, hypothesis ranking, or later transfer.
TECAN-style resource rewards should be evaluator minted and tied to downstream value.

17 HiveForge: Collective Research and Engineering
17.1 Purpose
HiveForge is a capstone environment in which no single agent receives all information or capabilities

the collective rather than increasing message volume or verbal complexity.

17.2 Project composition
A generated project may combine:
• literature or document analysis;
• data cleaning and analysis;
• software implementation and testing;
• formal specification or proof;
• experiment design and execution;
• technical writing, editing, and source verification;
• security, governance, or deployment checks.

3. A specialist becomes unavailable and work must be reassigned.
4. One plausible intermediate artifact is subtly wrong and should be caught by peer review.
5. A summary omits provenance or a crucial caveat.
6. The project requires a strategic pivot after an experiment falsifies the leading hypothesis.
7. Several agents redundantly pursue the same branch unless ownership is explicit.
8. The most capable model is expensive and should be invoked only for high-value bottlenecks.

19 The Transfer Ring
19.1 Purpose
The Transfer Ring is not one additional environment. It is a protocol for generating structurally corresponding problems across domains. It tests whether the architecture learns reusable abstractions and
whether TransWeave or other transfer mechanisms correctly reject inappropriate reuse.

19.2 Cross-domain isomorphisms and near-isomorphisms
A hidden dependency-and-intervention structure may appear as:
• a key, river, and exit problem in AGI Maze;
• a crafting and resource problem in Neoterics;
• a door, docking, and energy problem in RoboGarden;
• a package and service migration in RepoOps;
• a lemma dependency in LeanGarden;
• a pathway intervention in Virtual Scientist;
• a commitment and authority problem in SocietyLab;
• a distributed critical path in HiveForge;
• a capability and policy dependency in SelfLab.
Some renderings should be truly transferable. Others should share superficial features while differing in a
crucial causal or normative relation.

19.3 Transfer measurements
• zero-shot and few-shot performance;
• reduction in adaptation episodes and compute;
• reuse of causal schemas, plans, options, programs, attention policies, and memory structures;
• representation invariance under renamed vocabulary, changed API, or changed modality;
• correct rejection of invalid mappings;
• negative-transfer cost and recovery;
• preserved provenance and scope of transferred artifacts;
• order sensitivity when learning and transfer are interleaved;
• bidirectional transfer between abstract and embodied renderers.

19.4 Selective transfer as the key standard
Unconditional reuse is not intelligence. A transfer mechanism should receive credit both for carrying
structure that remains valid and for marking components that must be relearned. False transfer admission
and false rejection should be reported separately.

20 The Real-Work Shadow Stream
20.1 Why real work is needed
Synthetic environments provide ground truth and clean attribution, but a system can overfit their style.
Candidate architectures should therefore run in shadow mode on actual OmegaHive tasks in literature
research, software development, mathematical formalization, experiment design, curation, writing, editing,
governance, and operations. These workloads are heterogeneous yet produce concrete artifacts [1].

20.2 Automated and external oracles
• code can be compiled, tested, profiled, and scanned;
• formal claims can be independently replayed;
• citations, quotations, and provenance can be checked;
• experiments can be rerun and results compared;
• requirements and document schemas can be validated;
• task ownership, cost, rework, latency, and escalation can be measured;
• deployment and incident procedures can be exercised in twins.
Open-ended scientific importance, conceptual novelty, and prose quality still require sampled expert
judgment. That judgment should be recorded with rubric, reviewer identity, disagreement, and calibration
where possible.

20.3 Shadow comparison
The candidate system receives the same task stream as the incumbent but cannot control consequential
outputs initially. It produces proposed plans, module routes, artifacts, confidence, and predictions. The
evaluator compares these with the live path, independent checks, and later outcomes. Promotion begins
only after stable shadow benefit is observed under a predeclared rubric.

20.4 Real-work learning
Completed Context Frames become precedents. Useful decompositions, proof obstacles, code repairs, sourcequality patterns, review criteria, and coordination policies may enter the curriculum. The architecture is
judged partly by whether these precedents reduce future rework rather than merely enlarge memory.

21.2 Cost-control techniques
• Run candidate and baseline on the same seeds, observations, model routes, and tool states.
• Cache stable tool or model outputs when testing orchestration rather than generation.
• Reuse the parent release’s baseline distributions until its dependencies change.
• Use sequential testing and stop early when benefit or regression is already decisive.
• Run affected cells plus a small random cross-suite audit rather than the complete ecology on every
commit.
• Use trace replay and R0 robotics before live environment execution.
• Use exact causal ablations sparsely and recalibrate cheap impact surrogates periodically.
• Reserve frontier models and sensor-rich simulation for late gates.
• Record tail latency, cost, and failure rather than reporting means alone.
• Cap communication and model calls explicitly in multi-agent environments.
• Separate environment-generation cost from candidate inference cost.

21.3 Coverage matrix
Every enabled cognitive process should have at least:
• one direct target test;
• one interaction or synergy test;
• one stress, fault, or adversarial test;
• one retention or transfer test;
• one resource-efficiency measurement;
• one causal intervention or oracle-substitution test where applicable.
A pairwise covering array across domain, cognitive demand, novelty type, hive topology, authority, and
budget can provide broad coverage without enumerating the full Cartesian product.

22 Promotion, Shadowing, Fallback, and Rollback
22.1 Lexicographic and Pareto-aware promotion
A candidate should not be promoted because one weighted sum increased. The decision order is:
1. Hard invariants: permissions, evidence identity, counterfactual quarantine, exact formal authority,
and rollback viability.

39

The OmegaHive ProtoAGI Test Suite

August 2026

2. Protected anchors: no material regression in unrelated core capabilities, calibration, or open-ended
behavior.
3. Target benefit: the predicted useful effect exceeds a predeclared practical threshold.
4. Fresh generalization: the effect survives new seeds and at least one structural holdout.
5. Causal attribution: the expected process signature and ablation result are present.
6. Developmental value: retention, transfer, or knowledge dividend does not materially degrade.
7. Economics: incremental utility justifies additional compute, latency, memory, communication, and
human burden.
8. Operational maturity: shadow results, fallback behavior, monitoring, and recovery meet the risk
tier.

22.2 Illustrative machine policy
PROMOTE only if
hard_invariants == PASS
and protected_anchor_regressions <= allowed_regression
and P(target_delta > minimum_useful_delta) >= threshold
and structural_holdout == PASS
and causal_signature == PRESENT
and developmental_regression == ACCEPTABLE
and verified_utility_gain / incremental_cost >= required_ratio
and fallback_and_rollback == TESTED

Thresholds are versioned and risk-specific. This is a policy structure, not a universal statistical recipe.

22.3 Ambiguous outcomes
An ambiguous result should usually lead to continued shadow operation, a narrower authority, a reduced
scope, or a targeted discriminating experiment. It should not be forced into a binary success narrative.

22.4 Staged authority
A useful default ladder is:
1. offline or replay-only evaluation;
2. shadow mode with no influence on live outcomes;
3. advisory mode visible to the incumbent controller;

4. bounded routing control over reversible actions;
5. primary control with fallback;
6. expanded authority only after post-promotion monitoring.
Learned columns, templates, options, memory policies, reasoners, schedulers, and whole PRIMUS components should all use the same conceptual pathway.

22.5 Post-promotion monitoring
Promotion is not the end of evaluation. The system should monitor distribution shift, cost drift, calibration,
fallback frequency, errors on protected anchors, new interaction effects, and capability-belief accuracy. A
rollback creates a new continuity record; it does not erase the failed episode or its external side effects.

23 Testing the Evaluators
23.1 Why evaluator validation is necessary
The suite can itself be benchmark-hacked through poor metrics, leakage, unstable generators, or a scoring
rule that rewards activity over value. Evaluator code and data therefore require versioning, tests, positive
and negative controls, shadow comparison, and retrospective analysis on archived runs.

23.2 Required controls
Each mature environment should contain or support:
• a known useful intervention;
• a useless but highly active intervention;
• a redundant reasoning or communication loop;
• an overconfident but inaccurate predictor;
• a component that spends more compute for the same result;
• a shortcut that breaks provenance or evidence identity;
• a policy-violating strategy that superficially improves task reward;
• a memorizing agent that exploits fixed seeds or surface templates;
• a conservative agent that avoids all risk but fails the task;
• an evaluator leak that should be detected by canaries.
An evaluator is not mature until it separates these controls reliably.

23.3 Generator and score versioning
Every run manifest should record generator version, scoring version, environment image, data snapshot,
model route, tool versions, policy, random seeds, and candidate code. Historical scores must not be silently
rewritten after evaluator changes. When a score changes, both old and new evaluator results should be
retained with an explicit migration note.

23.4 Leakage and canary tests
Promotion generators should include hidden canary mechanics, misleading but noncausal metadata, and
randomized identifiers. If candidate performance collapses when metadata is permuted or if it predicts
private evaluator fields, the run should be quarantined.

23.5 Human and LLM judge calibration
Where expert judgment is needed, use paired or blinded review, clear rubrics, disagreement records, and
periodic calibration against artifacts with stronger ground truth. LLM judges should be compared against
deterministic checks and human reviewers and should not control promotion alone.

31 Conclusion
The OmegaHive ProtoAGI Test Suite should be implemented as a living validation ecology rather than a
static benchmark collection. Its routine kernel is AGI Maze, because a low-bandwidth partially observable
world provides an unusually clean test of persistent latent state, belief tracking, memory, active observation,
and planning. AGI Maze is extended through hidden mechanics, stochasticity, developmental seasons,
motives, and multi-agent coordination, and is treated as one renderer of a more general latent-world engine.
The kernel is surrounded by Neoterics for broader causal and social ecology, RoboGarden for continuous
embodiment, RepoOps for useful digital work, LeanGarden for exact formal reasoning, Virtual Scientist for
causal discovery, SocietyLab for explicit social cognition, HiveForge for collective intelligence, and SelfLab
for evidence-grounded self-knowledge and governance. The Transfer Ring tests whether abstractions move
selectively among these worlds, while the real-work shadow stream tests whether synthetic gains survive
actual OmegaHive research and engineering.
Every architectural change enters with a predeclared evaluation contract. Every consequential action
can carry a committed prediction. Every result separates objective receipts from self-assessment. Every
important cognitive process is tested through matched variants, developmental trajectories, order tests,
protected anchors, causal interventions, and resource accounting. Hidden experiences become curriculum
after scoring, and their delayed usefulness is measured through a knowledge dividend. Promotion is staged,
hard-invariant-first, Pareto-aware, reversible where technically possible, and continuously monitored.
The suite does not attempt to prove AGI with one score. It creates a disciplined way to accumulate
evidence that an OmegaClaw or OmegaHive is becoming broader, more adaptive, more cumulative, more
synergistic, more metacognitively accurate, more collectively productive, more resource efficient, and
more governable. That is the form of evidence needed for coding agents to modify a complex proto-AGI
architecture rapidly without turning rapid iteration into benchmark gaming or architectural self-deception.
